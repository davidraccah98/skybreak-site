Same player, two goals

Two ways to play above your level

Rank says where you are. Skybreak shows how far above your level you play, and lets you compete on it. The same 14 challenges everywhere: what you learn in training counts in contests.

Personal · free

Learn where you beat your tier

Sign up a game with a champion and role. After it, every challenge of that champion and role is judged against the average player of your own tier.

  • Intelligence: your strengths and weaknesses per champion, and everyone's thresholds
  • Training with free improvement contests of 20 players
  • Milestones on the challenges you usually miss
Contests · prizes

Compete on your schedule

No brackets, no waiting for organizers, no team to schedule. Lock a champion, a role and the 3 challenges you are strongest at, play your normal solo queue game, and climb the leaderboard.

  • 20 players per contest, from any tier
  • Everyone is measured against their own tier: Bronze and Challenger on equal terms
  • Tickets of 2, 5 or 10 €: the top 4 win up to twice their ticket, places 5 to 12 get it back

Lock in, then just play

How a game works

  1. Lock your pickChampion and role, and in contests your 3 challenges, before the game starts. After that, nothing changes.
  2. PlayNo overlay, no pop-ups, nothing on screen. Just your game.
  3. Get judged automaticallySkybreak finds your game through Riot's official API and judges it against your tier, champion and role. Only the games you sign up.
  4. See where you standYour report and your improvement in training, or your place on the contest leaderboard.

From a player, for players

Why we built it

I have played League since I was 15 and climbed to Diamond 2 on a computer that ran it at 25 fps. Today I study and work, and I'm way worse. Most players are where I am: they love the game and want to improve and compete, but can't organize their life around it. Skybreak is what I wanted back then, and still want today. — the founder

Where we're going: players who improve earn points for coaching, and later a path to try-outs and a Skybreak team.
